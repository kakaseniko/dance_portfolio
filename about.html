Roots

What interests me is movement. Not just dance, but the many ways bodies move, adapt, interact with and respond to their surroundings. The similarities between seemingly unrelated practices, the differences, the unexpected connections, and all the questions that emerge somewhere in between.

My own exploration started with gymnastics in childhood and shifted towards dance at the age of 12. Hip hop and its related street styles became my biggest inspiration. Over the years, I explored choreography, modern and contemporary dance, before becoming drawn to freestyle, mostly breaking, hip hop, house and popping.

At some point climbing entered the picture and eventually became a major part of my life. Across around ten years of experience, including a five-year break from dance, I explored movement from a different perspective, also working as a boulder setter and creating movement challenges for others. Surfing, skateboarding and winter sports have further shaped my fascination with how movement changes depending on the environment, the constraints and the possibilities.

What now

My recent way back to dance came through contact improvisation, followed by a return to freestyle hip hop, its jams, battles and community. I'm now deliberately making more space for dance (and less for climbing), pursuing a childhood dream of becoming a dancer whatever kind of artistic practice that means.

Alongside this, I work part-time as a software engineer and data scientist, busy with research and development projects. I enjoy thinking across disciplines and finding ways to bring seemingly unrelated tools and perspectives together: What would it mean to dance on a climbing wall? Could data visualisation reveal patterns of interaction and energy in a hip hop jam? What happens when the somatic principles of contact improvisation meet the individuality, and rhythms of hip hop freestyle?

Looking forward

I would like to give more space to developing experiments into performances, running research projects and collaborations, and to finding new ways to explore, communicate and experience movement.

For me, this is about curiosity, fun, and connection: questioning familiar ways of moving, discovering unfamiliar ones, and creating spaces where people can experiment together.
